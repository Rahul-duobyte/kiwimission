Executive Snapshot

A Highland Opportunity with National Relevance

Arunachal Pradesh is building a future-ready kiwi economy founded on natural strengths, progressive growers and expanding horticultural capabilities. The vision connects high-altitude farms with quality planting material, scientific production, aggregation, value addition, reliable logistics and premium markets.

Arunachal Pradesh accounts for more than half of India's kiwi production, according to the value-chain assessment.
  • Sustainable livelihoods for hill communities
  • Higher productivity and better-quality fruit
  • Stronger farmer institutions and rural enterprises
  • Scientific post-harvest management
  • Greater participation of SHGs, FPOs and local youth
  • Wider domestic market access and progressive export readiness
  • Climate-responsive and responsible horticultural growth

This transformation reflects the spirit of Viksit Bharat @2047, combining local potential, modern infrastructure and market-led development for resilient rural prosperity.

The Highland Edge

Why Arunachal Kiwi Stands Apart

Natural advantage · distinctive quality · value-addition potential

Kiwi thrives across the state's temperate and high-altitude landscapes. Sloping terrain, adequate chilling and rainfall create a favourable environment for fruit development. Principal belts include the Ziro landscape of Lower Subansiri and the temperate areas of West Kameng.

  • Naturally grown in high-altitude conditions
  • Recognized for sweetness, juiciness and distinctive flavour
  • Rich in Vitamin C, Vitamin E, fibre, potassium and antioxidants
  • Suitable for fresh consumption and products such as juice, candy, pulp and dried kiwi
  • Harvest availability when supplies from some competing origins begin to recede
  • Aligned with demand for nutritious, premium and responsibly produced fruit
Organic recognition: Kiwi grown in Ziro Valley received organic certification in 2020 under the organic value-chain development framework for the North-East.

Orchard Establishment

Propagation and Planting Protocol

A compact implementation sequence for field teams and growers

Propagation through cuttings

  • Use cuttings approximately 0.5–1.0 cm thick, with short internodes and 15–30 cm length.
  • Treat cuttings with IBA at 4,000–5,000 ppm for 10 seconds.
  • Plant in a mist chamber using sand as the rooting medium.
  • Provide intermittent mist at 10-minute intervals.
  • The source reports 70–75% rooting through this method, commonly adopted in Arunachal Pradesh.
